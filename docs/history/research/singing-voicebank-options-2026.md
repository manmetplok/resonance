# Keep DiffSinger, rethink the voices and licences

> Archived research (2026-10-07). Its decisions live in
> [intents/compose/vocal-mcp-control-and-quality.md](../../intents/compose/vocal-mcp-control-and-quality.md)
> and [intents/compose/soulx-singer-backend.md](../../intents/compose/soulx-singer-backend.md).
> Source notes: [singing-voicebank-options-2026-notes/](singing-voicebank-options-2026-notes/).

DiffSinger is still the right engine for Resonance in late 2026, but the three voicebanks it ships with are no longer a safe default. No other open engine combines note-accurate MIDI+lyrics input, English, ONNX export and a working Rust path the way DiffSinger does, and upstream is active (v2.5.1 in January 2026, commits through September 2026). The problems are with the banks. TIGER has been frozen since April 2024. Meiji has a newer build (v170) than the v160 Resonance ships. Lilia MM 2.8 is still LIEE's newest release, but its terms of use ban "AI-generated music and other generative AI contexts", which describes Resonance's agent-driven vocal workflow almost exactly. None of the three, and none of the openvpi reference vocoders, may be bundled in an MIT/Apache distribution. Only Lilia lets you use rendered songs commercially without asking first. Of the three, only Lilia and Meiji respond to any expression curve (tension). The one notable new model is SoulX-Singer (February 2026). Its code and weights are Apache-2.0, it was trained on about 20,000 hours of English singing, and it takes a MIDI+lyrics score, which makes it the only credible replacement candidate. It is PyTorch-only with no ONNX export and no published CPU numbers, so for now it is a prototype to try, not a drop-in replacement. The recommendation is to keep the engine and add voices, not replace: upgrade Meiji to v170, run a listening test of LUNAI's English singers and Peiton, move the default voice off Lilia until julieraptor approves agent use, ship every bank and vocoder as a user install, and start a SoulX-Singer feasibility spike.

## The three banks: one stalled, one superseded, one legally awkward

**TIGER v106 (2024-04-29) is the last release, and the repo has not been pushed since** ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/spicytigermeat/tiger_diffsinger/releases)). v106 moved to Reflow and improved the pitch models ([v106 notes](https://github.com/spicytigermeat/tiger_diffsinger/releases/tag/v106)), and the README still says "latest v102". Nothing marks it deprecated, but it never picked up openvpi's later work: the LYNXNet backbone, the PC-NSF-HiFiGAN 2025.02 vocoder and the v2.5.1 language-embedding fix. One local finding matters: TIGER's acoustic model is a shared multi-speaker model with about 36 speaker embeddings. Besides the seven TIGER modes it includes Canary, Leif, Miyo, PJS and other community voices that were trained into it (local `dsacoustic/dsconfig.yaml`). Those voices belong to other creators. Resonance should not expose them as TIGER voices without checking their terms.

**Lilia MM 2.8 "JubiLIEE 2025" (2025-08-07, repacked as 1.1) is still the newest LIEE release.** It added "more English high tension data" ([MM2.8 release](https://github.com/julieraptor/DIFFSINGER-LIEE-Immortal-Idol/releases/tag/MM2.8)). It has no voice modes. The README tells users to get expression from tension, and notes that the DIFFS EN phonemizer does not produce `[dx]` or `[ax]` ([README](https://github.com/julieraptor/DIFFSINGER-LIEE-Immortal-Idol)). The missing English `v`, which Resonance works around by substituting `f`, is not mentioned anywhere upstream. No fix or successor has been announced on the public channels we could search; LIEE's Discord and Bluesky were out of reach.

**Meiji is the only one of the three with an upgrade.** LUNAI's bulk release "170" (2025-12-25) ships `Gahata_Meiji_v170.zip`, the most-downloaded LUNAI bank at 3,389 downloads, alongside 19 other singers. The release notes are empty, so what changed is unknown ([LUNAI release 170](https://github.com/lunaiproject/lunai_singers/releases/tag/170)). Meiji has four modes (Standard, Phantom/whisper, Lilith, Hunter) and covers 12 languages with phonemizers ([VocaVerse announcement](https://vocaverse.network/threads/gahata-meiji-diffsinger-release.10205/)). One technical risk applies to it. DiffSinger v2.5.1 disclosed that multi-dictionary language embeddings "never worked correctly since its release", because language IDs were all zero ([v2.5.1 release](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/releases/tag/v2.5.1)). Meiji uses `use_lang_id: true`, and both v160 and v170 predate the fix, so its language conditioning may be weaker than its config suggests. That is unconfirmed, since LUNAI may train with its own setup. Lilia (`use_lang_id: false`) is not affected.

## Expression controls are thinner than the variance pipeline suggests

The local configs show a gap between what the banks predict and what actually changes the sound. TIGER ships duration and pitch models but no variance model, and its acoustic model takes none of energy, breathiness, voicing or tension. Lilia's variance model predicts all four, but its acoustic model reads only tension. Meiji predicts and reads only tension. So **across all three banks, energy and breathiness curves have no effect on the output**. The only working controls are tension (Lilia, Meiji), gender/key-shift, speed, and the seven TIGER modes and four Meiji modes. DiffSinger added voicing and tension in v2.3.0 ([releases](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/releases)), so the engine supports richer control than these banks were trained for. A bank chosen for expressiveness should be checked for `use_energy_embed`/`use_breathiness_embed` in its acoustic config before Resonance shows those curves for it.

The vocoders also differ. TIGER and Meiji use tigermeat's own `tgm_hifigan`. Lilia bundles a fine-tuned NSF-HiFiGAN and names `pc_nsf_hifigan_44.1k_hop512_128bin_2025.02` as its default, the vocoder openvpi made standard in v2.5.1 ([SingingVocoders](https://github.com/openvpi/SingingVocoders/releases)). Resonance already prefers a bank's own vocoder over a shared one, which is right. It should also record which vocoder rendered each take, because their output differs.

The new zero-shot models do not close this gap. SoulX-Singer takes an external F0 curve in its melody-control mode, so Resonance could keep its own pitch, vibrato and curve editing and pass F0 in. But no new model documents energy, breathiness or tension inputs; their expression comes from the reference clip or text prompts ([arXiv 2602.07803](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07803); [TCSinger 2](https://github.com/AaronZ345/TCSinger2)). For parameter-level control the benchmark is still closed: Synthesizer V Studio 2 scripts pitch points, vocal modes and consonant timing ([Dreamtonics scripting](https://resource.dreamtonics.com/scripting/index.html)).

## English DiffSinger voices are still few, and LUNAI is the active source

The DiffSinger Wiki's "supports English" category lists only six banks: Canary, Hanami Hoshino, Laru Mine, Miyo, Peiton and TIGER ([DiffSinger Wiki](https://diffsinger.miraheze.org/wiki/Category:Voicebanks_supporting_English)). No dated community ranking exists; that discussion happens on Discord, so any claim that one bank is "best" needs a listening test. Two candidates stand out for a CMU-G2P pipeline. **Peiton** (NebulaMeadow) takes ARPAbet input natively and has seven modes, from a base "Americano" to operatic "Mocha" and power "Spiced". Commercial use needs a purchased licence ([UTAU Repository](https://utaurepository.miraheze.org/wiki/Peiton)). **LUNAI's English singers**, Katyusha (soprano/alto), Keiro Revenant (tenor) and Liam Thorne (baritone/bass), cover a full SATB range. They use the same packaging as Meiji, which Resonance already loads. A third-party choir-rehearsal app chose exactly these three as its English voices and downloads them at setup rather than bundling them ([sjespersen/singer](https://github.com/sjespersen/singer)). LUNAI is also the most active producer: 20 singers at v170, and an OpenUtau fork with a "Singer Hub" for installing banks, released July 2026 ([OpenUtau-lunai](https://github.com/keirokeer/OpenUtau-DiffSinger-Lunai)). Community threads also suggest Raine Rena, Hoshino Hanami and Nishiren Gard, but that comes from search snippets only, with no versions or terms ([UtaForum](https://utaforum.net/threads/looking-for-english-diffsinger-ai-voicebanks.25396/)).

For multi-speaker use, the DiffSinger approach is many separately downloaded banks, each with its own terms. Zero-shot models change this. **SoulX-Singer** turns any reference clip into the singer, was trained on about 20,000 hours of English plus 20,000 hours of Mandarin, and encodes English as phonemes with word-boundary tokens. That is close to what Resonance's CMU G2P already produces, though its exact phone set is not documented ([arXiv 2602.07803](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07803)). Its self-reported English WER of 0.149 is below the ground truth's 0.197. That more likely means clean, ASR-friendly output than better diction than real singers, and no independent comparison against DiffSinger banks exists.

## Licensing decides what Resonance may ship, and the answer is: no voices

| Asset | Terms | Bundle in MIT/Apache app? | Commercial song output |
|---|---|---|---|
| DiffSinger code | Apache-2.0 | Yes | n/a |
| openvpi NSF/PC-NSF-HiFiGAN | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | No | Unclear whether share-alike reaches rendered audio |
| TIGER | CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 + Commons Clause | No | Paid licence (Ko-fi) |
| Lilia (LIEE) | Bespoke ToU | Not addressed; porting clause | Free with credit "LIEE", but no generative-AI use |
| Meiji and LUNAI singers | LUNAI ToU (2026-01-16) | No ("distributing models or their copies") | Written permission needed |
| Peiton | Bespoke | No | Paid licence |
| SoulX-Singer | Apache-2.0, code and weights | Yes | Yes (check the reference voice) |

Sources: [openvpi/DiffSinger](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger), [openvpi/vocoders](https://github.com/openvpi/vocoders/releases), [TIGER LICENSE](https://github.com/spicytigermeat/tiger_diffsinger), [LIEE ToU](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1t13WKc7VA2Q3G_MppznbAwhl3vLIL2MNPyjVN3kWDDg/edit), [LUNAI ToU](https://github.com/lunaiproject/lunai_singers), [Peiton](https://utaurepository.miraheze.org/wiki/Peiton), [SoulX-Singer](https://github.com/Soul-AILab/SoulX-Singer).

**The engine is permissive; the voices and the reference vocoder are not.** Another open-source DAW reached the same conclusion in September 2026, finding "no English voicebank under a clearly compatible license", and stopped there ([sourdaw #4608](https://github.com/jcosta33/sourdaw/issues/4608)). For Resonance this means following OpenUtau: banks and vocoders stay out of the repo and out of release artifacts, and the user installs them and accepts their terms ([OpenUtau wiki](https://github.com/openutau/OpenUtau/wiki/DiffSinger-support)).

Lilia needs a specific decision. Its render terms are the most generous of the three: commercial use without permission, with credit "LIEE". But the same ToU says "You cannot use LIEE in AI-generated music and other generative AI contexts", and asks anyone porting LIEE to another synthesizer to contact the creator ([LIEE ToU](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1t13WKc7VA2Q3G_MppznbAwhl3vLIL2MNPyjVN3kWDDg/edit)). Resonance loads Lilia directly rather than through OpenUtau, and drives it from an LLM over MCP, so it plausibly falls under both clauses. Lilia is also the default voicebank. An email to julieraptor (liee.immortal.idol@gmail.com) is cheap and settles the question either way. LUNAI's ban on "porting models to other engines … or modifying them" is less clear for a host that runs the unmodified ONNX files, but its commercial-use rule is not: songs need written permission. These terms also carry over to future features. Lilia forbids using its renders as input to RVC or Diff-SVC, so a voice-conversion stage could not legally be chained after it. The app should store per-bank flags for credit string, commercial use, VC allowed and generative-AI allowed.

## Runtime and integration: DiffSinger fits, SoulX-Singer would need a port

DiffSinger's ONNX contract has been stable since v2.4.0 (rectified flow, continuous acceleration, log-e mels). The master branch is now adding dual-timestep reflow and LYNXNet2, and a `v3` branch exists, so a future v2.6 or v3 tag could change exported model inputs and is worth watching ([openvpi/DiffSinger](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger)). OpenUtau, the reference host, is busy: near-daily alphas, real-time pitch generation added in September 2026, and DiffSinger G2P and language-ID fixes ([OpenUtau](https://github.com/openutau/OpenUtau)). Its renderer splits into pitch, variance, duration and acoustic+vocoder stages with a per-phrase cache ([source dir](https://github.com/openutau/OpenUtau/tree/master/OpenUtau.Core/DiffSinger)), which is the layout to copy for near-interactive re-rendering.

Resonance's own setup has three findings that need action. **Speed:** it renders at `speedup` 20, which is 2.5 times the denoising steps of OpenUtau's default of 50 ([OpenUtau wiki](https://github.com/openutau/OpenUtau/wiki/DiffSinger-support)). No published CPU real-time-factor data exists, so measure the quality loss at 50 with the existing `render_resonance_vocal` example and consider exposing a quality setting. **ROCm:** ONNX Runtime **removed the ROCm execution provider in 1.23** ([ORT docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/ROCm-ExecutionProvider.html)), so the `rocm` feature in `resonance-svs/Cargo.toml` points at a provider that no longer exists. ort falls back to CPU without saying so unless `.error_on_failure()` is set ([ort EP docs](https://ort.pyke.io/perf/execution-providers)), which means a "GPU" build may already be running on CPU. Move to MIGraphX and log which provider is active. **ort version:** ort 2.0.0-rc.13 (2026-07-28) is a low-risk bump from the pinned rc.12, but its CUDA provider expects CUDA 13.2 or later ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/ort/latest)).

SoulX-Singer is the opposite case: a strong model with no integration path yet. It is PyTorch on Python 3.10, publishes no ONNX export, and documents neither VRAM nor CPU needs. Its own README recommends correcting alignment by hand in its MIDI editor ([GitHub](https://github.com/Soul-AILab/SoulX-Singer)). Integrating it means either a Python sidecar or an in-house ONNX export of its encoder, DiT and vocoder plus a flow-matching sampler in Rust. That is plausible, since the DiffSinger path already runs a diffusion loop, but it is real work.

The rest of the field does not fit:

| Candidate | Why it does not fit |
|---|---|
| VocalRender (2026 naturalness leader on paper) | Evaluated in Mandarin only ([arXiv 2607.27768](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27768)) |
| Vevo2 | Weights are CC-BY-NC-ND ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/RMSnow/Vevo2)) |
| TCSinger 2 | Ships no clear pretrained checkpoints |
| YingMusic-Singer | Needs a sung audio guide, not MIDI |
| ACE-Step 1.5, YuE, DiffRhythm | Song generators conditioned on lyrics, style, BPM and key, not a note-level melody; need 4–16 GB of VRAM ([ACE-Step 1.5](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5); [DiffRhythm](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/DiffRhythm)) |

Song generators could at most be an optional "generate a demo" job. For a voice-conversion layer, Applio/RVC is MIT and exports to ONNX ([Applio](https://github.com/iahispano/Applio)). Seed-VC is GPL-3.0 and was archived in November 2025 ([seed-vc](https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc)).

## Conclusion

The picture has changed in one way since these banks were chosen. Sound quality and engine choice are no longer the weak point; licensing and provenance are. Resonance can ship the engine with a clean MIT/Apache licence, but every voice it can currently play comes from a hobbyist creator whose terms either forbid redistribution, require permission for commercial songs, or (in Lilia's case) appear to forbid the very agent-driven workflow Resonance is built around. In order:

1. Keep DiffSinger and make every bank and vocoder a guided user install, with each bank's terms shown and its credit string stored.
2. Ask julieraptor about agent use, and switch the default voice until there is an answer.
3. Upgrade Meiji to v170 after diffing the zip.
4. Run a blind listening test of Katyusha, Keiro Revenant, Liam Thorne and Peiton against the current three. Check each acoustic config for live energy and breathiness embeddings.
5. Fix the ROCm feature and measure `speedup` 50.

Over the longer term, the only way to bundle a voice in Resonance is a permissively licensed model with a permissively licensed reference voice. SoulX-Singer's Apache-2.0 weights make that possible for the first time. A time-boxed spike measuring its CPU cost, English diction and F0-in control, run as a Python sidecar before any ONNX port, is the highest-value experiment available. If it holds up, the remaining legal question is no longer about the model but about whose voice the reference clip is: ideally a recording Resonance owns.
